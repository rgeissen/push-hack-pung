The open instrument platform

Open music modules, at home on every device.

Pung is what makes them at home. It hosts them inside your DAW as devices of its own, gives every one a designed interface on the hardware under your hands, and lays modulation, sounds and power management over all of them. One stack, built to play the same wherever you make music, starting on Ableton Push 3 Standalone with {{N:modules}} modules.

The interface

One design, two levels

Pung defaultEvery module gets it. Pung reads a module's controls and designs its pages from them: knobs, lists, and the pictures it recognises (envelopes, filters, LFOs), each moving with the sound. A module nobody has drawn a screen for is still a finished instrument.
Pung customWhere an instrument deserves its own: a front panel in that instrument's design language, shipped only when it is substantially better than the default and true to the instrument. {{N:custom}} modules have one so far.

Both follow one published set of rules: the same controls in the same places, the same motion, a time budget for every frame. Read the design

A host inside your DAWEvery module is a device like the DAW's own: in its browser, played, automated and saved with your project. On a Push that is Ableton Live, and nothing new to learn.
One instrument, two surfacesThe same sections under your hands and in your browser: what the hardware shows is a section of the instrument's web page, with the same pictures and the same state.
Modulation over everythingTwo LFOs, the wheel and pressure on any control of any module, whoever made it, and saved with your project.
Performance, governedPung measures what every module costs and lets an emulator rest when it is silent, so the processor and the battery go to what you hear.
Deployed, not assembledOne installer, one signed pack of modules, and every Pung you run at a glance on pung.live: its health, its modules, its updates.
Open and freeEvery module is built from its own source and keeps its licence. Pung itself is open source.
