Stacks

A sound, with everything it stands on.

A stack is the modules you play, in their order, each with its state, and Pung's modulation over them. Take one from a deployment (a Pung that runs somewhere: today on a Push), keep it here, send it to a deployment: the same one next month, or another.

A stack is the modules you play, in their order, each with its state, and Pung's modulation over them: the thing that travels from one Pung to another. Stacks arrive with the next Pung; the vision says where they go.

Your stacks are kept in this browser, and travel to your deployments over your home network. Nothing passes through the internet.

What travels

What a stack holds

  • The modules, in the order they play: a MIDI generator in front, an instrument, effects behind. A stack names them; it never carries them, and never firmware. A deployment installs what it lacks from the module pack.
  • The state of each, as the module itself keeps it: every control where you left it.
  • What Pung lays over them: its own modulation (LFO A and B, the wheel, pressure) on the controls you gave it.
  • What it needs, said before anything loads: the modules, the Pung that runs them, and firmware that must be yours.

A stack is a small file. Download one to back it up or to hand it to someone; drop a file on this page to add it.

Today, on a Push 3

One Pung device, whole

On a Push a stack is what one Pung device holds: an instrument with the MIDI effect in front of it and Pung's modulation, or a MIDI generator, or an audio effect. A stack you send appears in Push's own browser, under User Library › Presets › Instruments › Pung › Pung Stacks, and loads there like any preset: no computer, no internet.

Effects behind an instrument are devices of their own in Live and not yet part of one stack. That is next, with more kinds of hardware to send a stack to; the vision has the plan.
