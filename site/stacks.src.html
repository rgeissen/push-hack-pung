Stacks

Everything of Pung's in a Set, in one place.

A stack is what one Set uses of Pung: every Pung device in it, with its module and version, its sound, and what those need. Take it from a deployment (a Pung that runs somewhere: today on a Push), keep it here, and hold another deployment against it before the Set goes there.

A stack is what one Set uses of Pung: every Pung device in it, with its module and version, its sound, and what those need. It is how a Set will play the same on another Pung. Stacks arrive with the next Pung; the vision says where they go.

Your stacks are kept in this browser, and travel to your deployments over your home network. Nothing passes through the internet.

What it is

What a stack holds

  • Every Pung device of the Set, with the track it is on: instruments, audio effects, MIDI generators.
  • The module of each, and its version. A stack names modules; it never carries them, and never firmware. A deployment installs what it lacks from its module pack.
  • The sound of each, as the module itself keeps it, with the MIDI effect in front of an instrument and Pung's modulation over it.
  • What it needs, said before anything is missing on stage: the modules, the Pung that runs them, firmware that must be yours, and how many emulators play at once.

A stack is a small file. Download one to back it up or to hand it to someone; drop a file on this page to add it.

Today, between two Pushes

How a Set moves

  1. Take the stack of the Set while it is open on the Push it was made on.
  2. Check the other Push against it here. What it lacks is installed from its module pack with one press; Live restarts there on your word, and firmware you own is yours to add.
  3. Move the Set as you move any Set, and open it. Its notes, clips and automation are Ableton's and travel in the Set; every Pung device comes up with its sound.

The stack never replaces the Set, and Pung never reads or changes a Set file. One thing is still bound to the Push a Set was made on: automation and MIDI mappings of Pung devices follow that Push's order of modules. The next Pung gives every control one place for good; the vision has the plan.

One device

A patch

What one Pung device holds is a patch: an instrument with the MIDI effect in front of it and Pung's modulation, or a MIDI generator, or an audio effect. Take one from a deployment, and send it to one: it appears in Push's own browser, under User Library › Presets › Instruments › Pung › Pung Stacks, and loads there like any preset.
