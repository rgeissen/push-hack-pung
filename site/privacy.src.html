When you open a page or download a file

The server's log

pung.live is served from a server I run myself in Germany. There is no hosting company and no content delivery network in between.

As every web server does, it receives the address your request comes from (your IP address), the time, the page or file asked for, the page you came from if your browser says so, and your browser's description of itself. It may write these into a log that is used only to keep the server running and to notice attacks, and that is deleted when it is no longer needed for that. I do not combine it with anything else and do not try to find out who is behind an address.

The legal basis is Article 6 (1) (f) GDPR: my interest in a site that works and stays safe.

What the site does not do

No cookies, no tracking, nothing from elsewhere

  • It sets no cookies.
  • It counts no visitors and uses no analytics.
  • It loads no fonts, scripts, pictures or videos from other servers. The fonts are served from pung.live itself.
  • It has no accounts, no forms and no newsletter.

What stays with you

In your browser, on your network

Your deployments. When you connect a Push, your browser remembers its address and the name you gave it, so you need not type them again. This list is kept in your browser's storage on your computer. It is never sent to pung.live. When you switch to a deployment's own page with the control in the bar, your browser hands that page the list (names and home-network addresses) and the page of pung.live you came from, so it can offer your other deployments and the way back; this passes between your browser and your own deployment only.

Your stacks. Stacks you take are kept in your browser's storage as well, and in the files you download. They are not uploaded.

Light or dark. If you use the switch in the bar, your browser remembers which you chose, in its storage. It is never sent to pung.live.

Talking to your Push. The pages reach the Pung on your Push directly from your browser, over your home network; where your browser asks for your permission first (Chrome, Edge, Firefox), only after you gave it. The control in the bar asks each of your deployments whether it answers, to show that. What your Push answers (its modules, its processor and storage, what plays) is shown to you and goes nowhere else.

You can remove all of it at any time: disconnect and remove a deployment on the Deployments page, delete a stack on the Stacks page, or clear this site's data in your browser's settings.

Pung on your Push

What Pung asks pung.live

When your Push is online, Pung asks pung.live now and then for two small files: the current list of modules, and the number of the newest Pung. Module packages you install may be downloaded from pung.live too. The server sees these requests as it sees a visit to a page (above); Pung sends nothing about you, your Push or your music.

You can switch this off: Only the modules that came with this Pung, in Pung's settings, makes Pung ask pung.live nothing.

Other sites

Links and older downloads

Links to GitHub and to the modules' own pages take you to their operators, whose privacy rules apply there. Pung releases up to 0.5.0 and their sources are downloaded from GitHub. Push Hack, which the Pung Installer installs where it is missing, is downloaded from GitHub by the installer on your computer.

If you write to me by e-mail, I use your address and what you write to answer you, and keep the message only as long as the matter needs.
