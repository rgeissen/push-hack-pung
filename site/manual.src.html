Before you start

What you need

  • A Push 3 Standalone with Push Hack: push-manager, push-display and push-catalog, version 0.2.0-alpha or newer. That release brings the Live device extensions Pung is built on.
  • Push Hack's VST3 scan switched on: Push Manager, System tab.
  • The PushHack Remote Script in Live: Live's Settings › MIDI › Control Surface: PushHack. The Pung view follows Live's selection through it.
Which Push Hack

Pung needs Push Hack's Live device extensions (the vision page lists them). They are part of Push Hack since 0.2.0-alpha. With an older Push Hack, Pung's view does not open in place of Push's device view, and the pads do not stay with Live while it is open.

Setup

Install

Pung is in Push Hack Catalog. Install it there like any other hack.

  1. Open Push Hack Catalog in a browser on your computer or phone.

    In your browser
    http://push.local:7702

  2. Find Pung and choose Install. The Catalog downloads pung.tar.gz (76 MB, every module inside), installs the service and starts it.

  3. Restart the Push once. Live looks for new plugins only when it starts. After the restart, Pung's devices are in Push's browser and Pung is in Push Hack Home (Shift + Help).

Later versions arrive the same way: the Catalog shows an update when Pung has a new release. Devices in an open Set keep their state through the update.

Getting started

Your first Pung device

  1. Select a MIDI track and open Push's browser to add an instrument.

  2. Open User Library › Presets. Pung's devices are in Instruments › Pung (synths), Audio Effects › Pung (effects) and Instruments › Pung MIDI Effects (MIDI effects on a track of their own). Each entry also carries Live's own tags, such as Synthesizer › FM for Dexed or Reverb for CloudSeed, for browsers that filter by tag.

  3. Load one. With Push in its device view, the Pung view opens by itself. Play the pads: scales, layouts, Note Repeat and recording are Live's, as always.

Pung devices are ordinary Live devices. Put them in racks, automate them, send to them, save the Set: Live keeps every module's state with the Set. The modules run outside Live's process, so if one crashes, Live goes on and the device is silent until Pung has restarted it.

On the Push

Sounds

Every Pung device's sounds are browsed the same way, whichever module it runs: its own sounds (Factory), the ones you saved (User), your Favourites and what you chose lately (Recent). Open the device, then press SOUNDS (upper button 8) or the jog wheel. The browser works like Push's own.

ControlIn the sound browser
Jog wheel, turnMove on the level you are on: Source › Bank › Sound. A sound plays once you stop on it, so a quick spin plays only where it ends.
Jog wheel, pressOne level down. On a sound: keep it and close the browser.
Jog wheel, nudge leftOne level back up.
Jog wheel, nudge rightOn a sound, its menu: favourite, Save, Save As, Rename, Delete (which asks first).
Upper rowStraight to Factory, User, Favourites or Recent.
Encoder 1, Page < Page >The bank, category or expansion card.
Lower rowFav, Save, Save As, Rename, Delete, Compare, Cancel, Load. Hold Compare to hear the sound you had before you opened the browser.
  • Nothing is lost by looking. Cancel, or leaving the browser any way but loading, brings back the sound you had, exactly.
  • Naming: Save As proposes a name. To change it, turn the jog wheel for the letter at the cursor and nudge it to move the cursor, or turn the eight encoders, one letter each. Press the jog wheel when done.
  • Your sounds are Schwung's own user presets, file for file: a sound saved on Move opens on Push, and the other way round.
  • Your sounds are in Push's own browser too: User Library › Presets › Instruments › Pung › Mini-JV › your sound (and the same for every module). Loading one brings the module with that sound, already named.
  • Every sound has its name from the start. Where a module can only name the sound it has loaded, Pung reads the other names in the background the first time, without changing your device's sound; that takes a few seconds, once per module and bank.
  • The Pung view's header names the device's sound; a * in front means you changed it since.

On the Push

Pages and screens

A module's pages come from the module itself: its own sections and their names, in its order. Every parameter is on a page, and each device remembers the page you left it on.

Where a page holds something Pung recognises, it draws it as a picture over the encoders that set it, with each encoder's name and value below. Touch an encoder and its part of the picture lights up.

  • An envelope: its stages as one shape, whatever the module calls them (attack, decay, sustain and release; or a JV's T1 to T4, a DX7's R1 to R4).
  • A filter: its response curve from cutoff, resonance and type (low-pass, high-pass, band-pass or notch, and how steep).
  • An LFO: its wave, with more cycles the faster its rate.
  • A mix of sources: a fader over each level, and how much each source contributes to the whole.

On the Push

The sound, live

While a device plays, its pictures show what the sound is doing, not only how it is set. They follow one rule: nothing moves unless the sound moves, and nothing moves that Pung cannot know. Where Pung cannot know something, the picture stays still rather than guess.

One colour means "the sound, right now": the cyan blue of the marks below. Orange still means the encoder under your finger, and white is how the device is set.

  • Envelopes. Every note you play is a dot running along the envelope's lines in time: up the attack, down the decay, resting at the sustain point while you hold the key, and down the release once you let go. Under the envelope, a bar of its stages (A, D, S, R, or T1 T2 T3 S T4 on a JV) lights the stage each note is in; each stage is drawn as wide as its knob is turned. When a module gives its times in milliseconds or seconds, or Pung knows its engine (Mini-JV, Dexed), the timing is exact; otherwise Pung reads the time from the knob, and for the volume envelope it also listens: the dot waits at the top until the sound actually peaks, and the next notes run on what it heard. Effects' envelopes, such as a gate's, stay still: they follow the audio, not your notes; so do looping envelopes, which run on their own. Each new note briefly lights the envelope's start.
  • Filters. Behind the curve, in grey, is the actual sound: its spectrum, low notes on the left, high on the right. When the sound reaches higher than the curve, something else is opening the filter, such as its envelope or key follow.
  • LFOs. A dot runs along the wave at the LFO's real speed when Pung can know where the LFO is: when it is synced to Live's tempo with a rate like 1/8, or restarted by each note with a rate in Hz. An LFO that restarts on each note flashes at its start when you play. Any other LFO stays still.

Mini-JV runs in the JV-880's own time. Its Pitch, Filter and Amp Env pages show every note you play as a dot running through the JV's four stages, timed with the JV-880's own times, measured off the emulated instrument; the bar under them lights T1, T2, T3, S while you hold the key, and T4 once you let go. Dexed's operator and pitch envelopes run in the DX7 engine's own times the same way. Its Macros screen shows every one of its 28 voices: how loud each is, in the display, and where each one's filter is, on the TVF line, moving as the JV's envelopes and LFOs move them.

The pictures move while a note is held or the device sounds, for a moment after, and with Live's transport when a picture follows the tempo. The rest of the time the screen is drawn only when something changes. Most modules don't report where their envelopes and LFOs are, so for them the motion comes from the sound and the notes, and a free-running LFO stays still.

On the Push

Pung and Push's own view

Push's own device view edits every Pung device too: its Bank N is the module's own page N. The Pung view takes its place whenever a Pung device is selected and Push shows its device view.

  • Move to a track with a Pung device and the Pung view stays. Move to any other track and Push's own view is back.
  • Press Device in the Pung view to see the same device in Push's own device view (its Bank pages). Press Device again to return to the Pung view. This holds only while you stay on that device: select it again later and the Pung view opens as usual.
  • To keep Push's device view for good, switch Pung's DEVICE off in Push Hack Home (Shift + Help, choose PUNG, lower button 3), or on push-manager's Display page.
  • From Push Hack Home you can also open Pung by hand, on any track.

Playing

MIDI effects

Live on Push has no slot for a MIDI-effect plugin from another maker, so Pung's MIDI effects (the arpeggiators, chord tools and sequencers) reach a sound in two ways.

  • Inside a Pung instrument. Every Pung instrument has a MIDI FX selector on the pages after its own, one page per MIDI effect with that effect's settings. Pick one and it plays in front of the synth.
  • On a track of its own. Load a MIDI effect from User Library › Presets › Instruments › Pung MIDI Effects on its own track. On the track it should play, set Live's MIDI From to that track and the effect. This drives any instrument, Live's own included.

Beside the Push

The web page

Open http://push.local:7720 on a computer, tablet or phone on the same network (Push Hack Catalog may give Pung another port; push-manager's page lists it).

  • Devices: every Pung device in the Set, by track. A device's sections are the same as its pages on the Push, under the same names. Change a value on either side and the other follows within half a second; click a section's title and the Push turns to it.
  • Sound: the device's sounds, as in the sound browser on the Push: Factory, User, Favourites and Recent, the bank, and a search over every name. Click a sound or use the arrow keys to hear it, Enter or a double click keeps it, Esc brings back the sound you had. Each sound has a star, and yours can be renamed and deleted. Export downloads your sounds as Schwung preset files; Import adds such files, from another Push or from a Move.
  • Sounds (top of the page): your sounds for every module in one place, with a search over all of them; star, rename, delete, and Load on any Pung device in your Set running that module. Export all is one zip in the folder layout a Move keeps; Import sorts every sound of such a zip into its module.
  • Compartment pills: the device's sections as pills above its panel. Click one to show or hide its section, double-click one to show only that one, All to show every one. With one on, the Push turns to it; a dot marks the one the Push shows.
  • A module's own panel: modules with a web panel of their own (JE-8086, Mini-JV) show it there; the pills show and hide its sections too where the module supports it (Mini-JV).
  • Live: the sections' pictures move as they do on the Push (see The sound, live), and Mini-JV's panel shows its voices under the Macros. Only the sections you show are updated, and nothing is sent while the page is in a background tab.
  • Settings: Push display › Detail card on touch brings back Move's large card on every touch. It is off on the Push, where the touched cell already shows everything in place.

Beside the Push

Adding modules

The Modules page on Pung's web page lists every module that came with your Pung release: instruments, audio effects and MIDI effects, by kind and tag, each with its version, licence and source.

  1. Install the modules you want. They install from the copy inside Pung's release, on the Push itself: nothing is downloaded.

  2. Apply. New devices need one Live restart, because Live looks for plugins only when it starts. Apply saves nothing for you: save your Set first. Live restarts in about 30 seconds and recovers the Set. Install several modules, then Apply once.

  3. Add firmware where a module needs it. JE-8086, Mini-JV and Osirus emulate hardware and need its firmware, which Pung never ships. Add the file on the module's card; Pung recognises it by its checksum.

A new Pung release brings new modules and new versions; the Modules page shows updates. Removing a module needs Apply too.

During the test phase, modules come inside Pung's releases. After it, Pung's module pipeline, which is already built, will serve them directly, so updates arrive as Schwung publishes them.

Help

Removing Pung

Remove Pung with Push Hack Catalog. Its browser entries go with it. Its plugin stays on purpose, so Sets that use Pung devices still open, with those devices silent.

Delete the plugin (/data/.vst3/Pung.vst3) only when no Set uses it any more, and never just before Live recovers from a crash: Live replays the Set's history while it recovers, and a missing plugin crashes it.
